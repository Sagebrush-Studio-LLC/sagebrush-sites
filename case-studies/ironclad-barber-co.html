Concept Project · Case Study

Ironclad Barber Co.

A vintage-modern barbershop site built to prove the same underlying system can flex into a completely different brand register — and to give a walk-in-only shop a reason to be found online.

View live site → Back to portfolio

Problem

Barbershops are one of the service businesses most likely to skip a website entirely — many run on walk-in traffic and a bare Google Business listing. That works for existing regulars, but it loses anyone new to the area or new to the shop: no way to check pricing before walking in, no way to see the actual quality of cuts beforehand, and no sense of whether the shop's style (traditional vs. modern fades) matches what they want.

Approach

Ironclad needed to read as a completely different kind of business at a glance — and not just in palette. Instead of the card-grid menu and click-to-expand gallery used elsewhere in the portfolio, the whole layout is built around how a real barbershop actually presents itself: a full-bleed vintage hero photo with a badge-style crest mark instead of a text logo, a two-column ledger with dotted price leaders standing in for the paper price board taped to the mirror, and a horizontal scroll-snap filmstrip for the gallery — no modal, just swipe past the cuts like flipping through a lookbook. The About section drops the usual photo-and-copy split for a single pull-quote from the owner overlaid on a dim shop photo. Dark charcoal, brass, and rust with serif type carry the vintage-barbershop mood the rest of the way.

Tech Stack

Same offline-first foundation as the rest of the portfolio: plain HTML5, CSS3, vanilla JavaScript, single self-contained file, zero external dependencies, no build step. The gallery here uses native CSS scroll-snap instead of a JS lightbox — one less interaction pattern to maintain, and a better fit for a quick horizontal browse than a full-screen modal.

Result

No real client, no fabricated numbers.

Verified directly in-browser: zero external network requests, zero console errors, working filmstrip gallery, ledger menu, and mobile nav at phone widths. Worth noting as a real (if minor) finding from testing: an early pass hid the desktop nav links at the mobile breakpoint without providing a working menu to replace them — caught and fixed before it shipped. That's the kind of gap a real QA pass is supposed to catch before a client ever sees it.
